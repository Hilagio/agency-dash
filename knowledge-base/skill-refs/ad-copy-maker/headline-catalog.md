# Headline catalog

Patterns for each headline angle type, the sub-angles that live under them, and the rules a headline
has to obey. The slot each type belongs in is in `composition.md`; this page is about writing the
line.

Source: Headline Angle Catalog (the seven core types, the urgency and price rules) and
SOP – Write Compelling RSAs §1.

---

## The constraint

Thirty characters. Google truncates nothing below it and gives nothing above it, so the useful band
is **25–30 characters** — under about 20 the slot is being wasted (Headline Angle Catalog, the
30-character table; Headline Quality Checklist, "most headlines 25–30 characters").

Characters are counted the way the ad editor counts them: user-perceived characters, so an accented
letter, a combining mark or a non-Latin glyph counts once.

Headlines are assembled by Google in changing combinations, so **each one has to stand alone**. A
headline that only makes sense after another headline is a broken headline.

---

## The seven core types

| # | Type | What it does | Sub-angle examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Relevance Anchor | matches the query | keyword anchor, category anchor, location anchor |
| 2 | Value Proposition | what you offer plus the main benefit | core offer, outcome, mechanism |
| 3 | Problem/Pain | mirrors the searcher's frustration | frustration, symptom, failed alternative, cost of inaction |
| 4 | USP / Benefit | why you and not the next result | speed, ease, method, range, specialisation, fit |
| 5 | Social Proof | why to believe it | rating, customer count, volume, credential, authority, tenure |
| 6 | Risk Removal | why it is safe to act | guarantee, trial, returns, flexibility, no-commitment |
| 7 | Call-to-Action | what to do next | transactional, exploratory, educational |

Value Boosters — what else is included — is an angle in the offer document and is written into
headlines as a USP or a Value Proposition variant, tagged `value booster` in its sub-angle.

Tag every headline by **type and sub-angle**, never by slot number. Aggregation across a cluster and
across cluster generations is done on the tag.

---

## Type 1 — Relevance Anchor

Two methods. **Keyword insertion is the default**; use static only when a condition below rules it
out.

| Method | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Dynamic keyword insertion (default) | the ad group's keywords are all safe and readable when printed in the ad | `{KeyWord:Default Text}` |
| Static | brand or competitor terms (trademark policy), sensitive or regulated terms, misspellings or awkward keyword strings, or wording that must be controlled | write the ad group's theme as a headline |

Read the ad group's enabled keywords before choosing. One unsafe or unreadable keyword is enough to
choose static for that ad group.

The **default text has to stand on its own** — a specific category phrase, never a placeholder like
"our products". A weak default is the single most common way this slot fails. Google counts the tag
as its default text, so the default must fit in 30 characters; a keyword longer than the slot shows
the default instead.

A keyword-level customizer the advertiser already maintains can replace keyword insertion as the
anchor — see `composition.md`, Dynamic text.

The anchor is written per ad group in H1 and is not pinned (see `composition.md`, Pinning).

---

## Type 2 — Value Proposition

`[specific offer] + [primary benefit]`, or `[outcome] without [pain point]`.

Say the thing and the result of the thing. A value proposition that could be pasted into a
competitor's ad is not one.

---

## Type 3 — Problem/Pain

`[Tired of / Stop / End / No more] + [their problem]`, or `[thing] that actually [works]`, or
`[fix] + [specific symptom]`.

Use the searcher's own words. Where the account has voice-of-customer evidence, the phrasing comes
from there rather than from a template.

Keep the balance: an ad whose every headline is negative reads as a complaint. Pair problem
recognition with solution language in the same ad.

---

## Type 4 — USP / Benefit

`[unique approach] + [benefit]`, `[specialisation] + [outcome]`, or `[comparison] + [advantage]`.

The test is subtraction: remove the brand name and ask whether the line still points at one
supplier. If it points at all of them, it is not a USP.

---

## Type 5 — Social Proof

`[number or rating] + [context]`, or `[credential]`.

| Sub-angle | Shape |
|---|---|
| Rating | `[rating] from [count] reviews` |
| Customer count | `Trusted by [count]+ [audience]` |
| Volume | `[count]+ [things] delivered` |
| Authority | category authority or ranking |
| Credential | certification, membership, award |
| Tenure | `[years] years of [expertise]` |

Every number is a claim. Use only figures the account's own evidence supports, at the value the
evidence gives — where the offer document has resolved a conflict between two published figures, the
resolved figure is the only one that may appear.

---

## Type 6 — Risk Removal

`[guarantee or trial] + [terms]`, or `No [barrier] required`.

| Sub-angle | Shape |
|---|---|
| Satisfaction | `Love it or [outcome]` |
| Money back | `[period] money back` |
| Trial | `Try free for [period]` |
| Returns | `[period] returns` |
| No commitment | `No [barrier] required` |
| Easy exit | `Cancel [ease]` |

A term that is designed but not yet live is not claimable. Take the date from the offer document and
leave the term out until it passes.

---

## Type 7 — Call-to-Action

`[action verb] + [specific outcome] + [modifier]`.

Strength runs from bare instruction ("contact us", "submit") through orientation ("learn more", "see
pricing") to a specific outcome ("get your free quote", "start your free trial"). Reach for the
right-hand end.

Tone follows traffic temperature (SOP – Write Compelling RSAs §1.2): soft and educational for cold,
exploratory for warm, direct and transactional for hot.

---

## Modifiers

**Urgency is a modifier, not a type.** Attach it to another headline — "…today", "…ends Friday" —
and use it in **at most two headlines** (Headline Quality Checklist). Urgency everywhere is urgency
nowhere, and manufactured deadlines are a policy and trust risk.

**Price is situational.** A price headline earns its slot on transactional intent and competitive
pricing. Premium positioning leads with value instead.

---

## What disqualifies a headline

From the Headline Quality Checklist:

- It does not stand alone, or it is a fragment of another line.
- It repeats a claim another headline in the same ad already makes.
- It repeats a callout or sitelink verbatim.
- It leans on generic filler — "best", "quality", "leading" — with nothing specific behind it.
- It makes a claim the account cannot defend, or one every competitor could make equally.
- It talks about the advertiser rather than the searcher.
- It is over 30 characters.

---

## Angle coverage in a finished ad

The ad, taken whole, must contain a relevance anchor, a value proposition, a call to action, and
either social proof or risk removal. Cold and warm traffic also need a problem/pain headline; on hot
and branded traffic it is optional (Headline Quality Checklist, angle coverage).
