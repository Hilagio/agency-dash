### Purpose

Your offer is the foundation everything else amplifies. Google Ads can't scale a broken offer.

> ❓ **The big question:** Would your ideal customer feel stupid saying no to this?

This SOP helps you design or refine your offer before you write a single ad. 

**If your offer is commoditized, no amount of copywriting will save it.**

---

### What this SOP is NOT

This SOP does **not**:

- Teach how to *communicate* your offer in ads (See: [SOP – Craft Your Offer Angles](../sops/SOP – Craft Your Offer Angles.md))
- Teach how to write headlines or descriptions (See: [Headline Angle Catalog](../catalogs/Headline Angle Catalog.md))

- Function as a one-time exercise (offers should evolve based on market feedback)

### When to run this SOP

Run this SOP if **any** of the following are true:

- Conversion rates are consistently below industry benchmarks
- You're competing primarily on price
- Prospects ask "what makes you different?" and you struggle to answer
- Your offer sounds identical to competitors
- You're launching a new product, service, or campaign
- Sales cycles are longer than expected
- Lead quality is poor (price-focused, low intent)
- Your offer fails the [**Offer Audit Checklist**]<!-- TODO: link target missing --> (<12 checks)

### The "Irresistible Offer" Framework

An irresistible offer answers four questions:

| Question | Element | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Is this worth it?" | **Value** | Solves a real problem / delivers dream outcome |
| "Why you over others?" | **Uniqueness** | Can't be easily compared or commoditized |
| "Why now?" | **Urgency** | Creates reason to act immediately |
| "What if it doesn't work?" | **Trust** | Removes risk and doubt |

> 💡 **Recommended reading:**
> Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* provides an excellent deep-dive into offer construction.
> Core concept: your offer's value isn't just what you deliver, it's the perceived gap between the dream outcome and the effort/sacrifice/risk required to get it.
> Increase the dream outcome, decrease the friction.

## Offer anatomy by vertical

Each vertical has **different levers to pull**. What makes an offer irresistible for a SaaS product is **different** from what works for a local service business or an ecommerce store.

## 1️⃣ Lead Gen

**The challenge:** Your "offer" is often the lead magnet. The actual service comes later.

**Common mistake:** Asking for contact information without providing sufficient perceived value in return.

#### Offer components:

| Component | Weak version | Irresistible version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The ask** | "Contact us" | "Get your free [specific deliverable]" |
| **Perceived value** | Vague consultation | Tangible deliverable with stated value ("valued at €500") |
| **Specificity** | "We'll review your situation" | "30-minute strategy call + personalized action plan" |
| **Urgency** | None | "This week only" / "Limited to 10 spots per month" |
| **Risk removal** | None | "No obligation" / "No sales pitch, guaranteed" |

#### Lead Gen offer formula:

[Specific deliverable] + [Perceived monetary value] + [Timeframe/scarcity] + [Risk removal]

#### Examples by sub-vertical:

| Sub-vertical | Weak | Irresistible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Legal** | "Contact us for a consultation" | "Free 30-min case review + written assessment of your options (no obligation)" |
| **Home services** | "Request a quote" | "Free same-day quote + 10% off if you book this week. Price-match guaranteed". |
| **Marketing agency** | "Let's talk about your marketing" | "Free Google Ads audit (valued at €750) + 30-min strategy call. Limited to 5/month". |
| **B2B services** | "Schedule a demo" | "See ROI in 15 minutes: Free personalized demo + implementation roadmap" |

## 2️⃣ SaaS

**The challenge:** Reducing friction to first value. Users need to experience the benefit before they'll pay.

**Common mistake:** Asking for commitment (credit card, annual plan) before proving value.

#### Offer components:

| Component | Weak version | Irresistible version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trial structure** | "Start your trial" | "14-day free trial, no credit card required" |
| **Time to Value** | Self-service, figure it out | "Setup in 2 minutes" / "Done-for-you onboarding" |
| **Stacked value** | Just the software | Software + templates + training + support |
| **Risk removal** | 7-day refund | "Love it or get a full refund, no questions asked" |
| **Urgency** | None | "Lock in founding member pricing" / "Price increases [date]" |

#### SaaS offer formula:

[Free/low-friction entry] + [Fast time to value] + [Stacked bonuses] + [Extended risk removal]

#### Examples by sub-vertical:

| Sub-vertical | Weak | Irresistible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Project Management** | "Start free trial" | "Free for teams under 10. No limits. No credit card. Forever". |
| **CRM** | "Request a demo" | "Free 14-day trial + done-for-you data migration + 1:1 onboarding call" |
| **Marketing tool** | "See pricing" | "Start free. Only pay when you see results. Cancel anytime". |
| **AI Tool** | "Sign up" | "100 free credits. No credit card. See results in 2 minutes". |

> 💡 **The "Reverse Trial" concept:**
> - Instead of: "Try free, then pay"
> - Consider: "Start with full features, then decide what you need"
> This increases perceived value and stickiness.

## 3️⃣ Ecommerce

**The challenge:** You're often selling products that exist elsewhere. The product alone isn't the offer, the deal structure is.

**Common mistake:** Competing on price alone, or having no offer structure at all ("just buy the product").

#### Offer components:

| Component | Weak version | Irresistible version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Price framing** | "€49.99" | "€49 (was €79) - Save 38%" |
| **Bundling** | Single product | "Complete kit: Product + Accessory + Guide" |
| **Threshold incentives** | None | "Free shipping over €50" / "Free gift at €75" |
| **First-purchase hook** | None | "15% off your first order" |
| **Risk removal** | "Returns accepted" | "100-day trial. Don't love it? Full refund + free return shipping". |
| **Urgency** | None | "Sale ends Sunday" / "Only 12 left in stock" |
| **Social Proof** | None | "47,000+ happy customers" / "Rated 4.8/5" |

#### Ecommerce offer formula:

[Product + Bundle/Stack] + [Discount/Threshold] + [Extended guarantee] + [Urgency trigger]

#### Examples by sub-vertical:

| Sub-vertical | Weak | Irresistible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Furniture** | "Shop sofas" | "Design your sofa. Free fabric samples. 100-night trial. Free returns". |
| **Supplements** | "Buy now" | "Subscribe & save 25%. First bottle 50% off. 90-day money-back guarantee". |
| **Fashion** | "New arrivals" | "First order: 20% off + free shipping + free returns. Use code WELCOME". |
| **Electronics** | "Shop TVs" | "Price match + 2-year warranty included + free same-day delivery over €500" |
| **Home Improvement** | "Shop tools" | "Buy the drill, get the bit set free (€45 value). Pro rewards: 5% back on every order". |

#### Ecommerce-specific tactics:

| Tactic | How it works | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bundling** | Combine products for perceived value | "Complete starter kit" vs. individual items |
| **Threshold offers** | Increase AOV with incentives | "Free shipping at €50" / "Free gift at €100" |
| **Subscriptions** | Lock in recurring revenue + discount | "Subscribe & save 20%" |
| **First-purchase offers** | Lower barrier to first conversion | "15% off first order" |
| **Loyalty programs** | Reward repeat behavior | "Earn 5% back in store credit" |
| **Extended guarantees** | Reduce perceived risk | "365-day returns" / "Lifetime warranty" |
| **Samples/trials** | Let them try before full commit | "Free sample pack" / "Trial size for €1" |

## Common offer failures (and fixes)

| Failure | Why it happens | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Generic value prop** | Afraid to be specific | Narrow your ICP; speak to their exact problem |
| **No urgency** | Don't want to seem "salesy" | Real scarcity is ethical; fake scarcity is not |
| **Competing on price** | No differentiation | Stack value (bundles, bonuses, service) instead of discounting |
| **Weak guarantee** | Fear of abuse | Strong guarantees increase conversions more than refund abuse costs you |
| **No social proof** | Haven't collected it | Start collecting reviews/testimonials immediately; display prominently |
| **Asking too much too soon** | Want leads fast | Offer value before asking for commitment |

### FAQ

**Q: How do I know if my offer is "good enough" to start advertising?**

A: Run the [**Offer Audit Checklist**]<!-- TODO: link target missing -->. Score 12+ = proceed. Score 8-11 = fix gaps first. Score <8 = significant work needed before ad spend. Don't advertise a weak offer at scale. You're just paying to learn it doesn't convert 🙂.

**Q: What if I can't change the offer? (I'm just the ads person)**

A: Document the gaps and present them to the decision-maker with this framing: "Here's why conversions are struggling, and here's what would fix it". Use the [**Offer Audit Checklist**]<!-- TODO: link target missing --> as evidence. If they won't change the offer, set realistic expectations about what ads can achieve.

**Q: How often should I revisit this SOP?**

A: Revisit when: (1) conversion rates drop significantly, (2) a major competitor changes their offer, (3) you launch a new product/service, (4) market conditions shift, or (5) you've collected enough customer feedback to identify new angles.

**Q: What's the minimum viable offer?**

A: At bare minimum: clear value proposition + one differentiator + one form of risk removal. Without these three, you're competing purely on price or luck.

**Q: Can I run ads while fixing my offer?**

A: Yes, but at reduced spend. Use the data to validate which offer elements resonate. But don't scale until the offer passes the audit. You'll burn budget on a leaky funnel.

**Q: What if my client won't add a guarantee?**

A: Find alternative risk removal: free consultation, no-obligation quote, "see results before you pay", easy cancellation, transparent pricing. If they won't reduce risk at all, their conversion rates will reflect that.

**Q: How do I create urgency without being sleazy?**

A: Authentic urgency only: real capacity limits ("only 5 client spots/month"), real deadlines (seasonal pricing, cohort starts), real scarcity (limited inventory). If the urgency isn't real, don't fake it. It damages trust and brand.

**Q: What if I'm in a commoditized market where everyone's offer looks the same?**

A: Stack value until comparison becomes difficult. Bundle services, add bonuses, extend guarantees, specialize in a niche, or create a proprietary methodology. If you can't differentiate the *what*, differentiate the *how* or the *who*.
